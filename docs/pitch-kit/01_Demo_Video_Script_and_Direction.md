# SAMA: demo video script, direction and camera plan

Your part is the solution and the demo, about 3 minutes. Your teammates have already covered the problem, impact, benefits and innovation, so don't repeat them. Open with one line that hands over from them, then show SAMA working.

A 2-minute cut is marked at the end in case the total video limit is tight.

## The story in one sentence

A phone becomes a measuring instrument, a published rulebook decides the grade, and the farmer walks away with a receipt they can check themselves, even with no internet.

Say "instrument, rulebook, receipt" at the start and again at the end. It is the thread judges will remember.

## What to show, ranked by impact

1. **Airplane mode on, and everything still works.** It proves the offline claim in five seconds.
2. **The farmer's phone checks the receipt offline and gets the same grade.** Nobody else in the room will have this.
3. **Change one character in the code and it fails.** Tamper-evidence shown, not just claimed.
4. **Tap a rejected onion and see why**: "Blackening 34% above the 30% limit". This is the transparency the PS asks for, made visible.
5. **The rule-pack table.** The norms changed on 4 June and again on 30 July 2026, and the same measurements re-grade under each rule in one tap.
6. **Two codes draw the sacks.** Neither farmer nor officer picks the sample.
7. **Capture guard and auto-shutter.** The camera refuses a bad photo and says why in one sentence.
8. **The app switches to Hindi** (or Marathi, which fits Nashik better).
9. **Every onion in a heap gets outlined and counted.**
10. **The landing page runs the same model in any browser** with nothing to install.

If you only have time for five, show 1, 4, 2, 3 and 5.

## Before you shoot

**Props**
- Two Android phones with the SAMA APK installed (sama.apk, v0.3.1). Phone A is the officer's, phone B is the farmer's.
- The printed calibration mat, from the app's "Print the mat" option. If you can't print it, use a plain white A4 sheet or a ₹5 coin for scale.
- 1 to 2 kg of onions, deliberately mixed: a few clean ones, a few with black mould on the neck, one soft or rotting one, one sprouted one, and one very small one.
- A plain dark cloth or a tray, a ruler or vernier caliper, and a kitchen scale if you have one.

**Phone settings**
- Brightness at maximum.
- Do Not Disturb on.
- Notifications cleared.
- Battery above 60%.
- Dark wallpaper.
- The app's language set to English (you switch it on camera later).
- One demo lot already completed, as a backup in case something fails live.

**Rehearsal.** Run the whole flow twice before you record. The first run on a phone after install is slower because the models load.

## Camera plan (cinematography)

You need three sources, and all three are free.

| Source | What it shows | How |
|---|---|---|
| A. Screen recording of phone A | The app, crisp | Android's built-in screen recorder (Quick Settings → Screen record, with the microphone off) |
| B. Top-down "hands" camera | The mat, the onions and your hands holding phone A | A third phone on a stand or a stack of books, pointing straight down, 1080p 30 fps, locked exposure |
| C. Talking-head camera | You, speaking to camera | A laptop webcam or phone at eye level. Window light should be in front of you, not behind you |

Record screen B's recording of phone B as well for the verification moment.

**Framing the edit (16:9, 1080p).** Put the phone screen recording as a tall panel on the right third, and the hands camera or talking head on the left two-thirds. Judges then see both the real onions and the exact screen. Don't show phone screens by filming them with another camera if you can avoid it, because glare and moiré look cheap.

**Light.** Soft daylight, near a window, around 10 am or 4 pm. Avoid direct sun on the onions: the capture guard will refuse glare, which is correct, but you want it to refuse once on purpose, not every time.

**Sound.** Voice matters more than video. Record the voice-over separately in a quiet room with a phone held 15 cm from your mouth (a voice-memo app is fine), or clip a wired earphone mic to your collar. Hang a blanket behind you if the room echoes.

**Movement.** Keep the camera still and let the hands move. Use slow, deliberate taps, and pause half a second on each result screen so viewers can read it.

**Editing** (CapCut or DaVinci Resolve, both free):
- Burn in captions, because many reviewers watch on mute.
- Add a small SAMA logo top-left.
- Zoom in slightly (110 to 120%) on the key numbers.
- Use no transitions except simple cuts.
- Keep background music low (−25 dB) and remove it under important lines.

## Filming at the vegetable shop

Yes, go. Real shop footage beats a table at home, and it answers "does it work on real onions?" before a judge asks. Do it like this:

1. **Ask first.** Tell the owner it's a student project for Smart India Hackathon, that it takes about 20 minutes, and that you won't show customers. Offer to buy 2 kg of onions. Most shop owners will say yes to that.
2. **Don't film customers' faces**, and don't film the owner without their okay. If they agree to appear, get a short verbal okay on camera ("Is it okay if we use this video for our college project?").
3. **Don't present SAMA as official.** Say it is a prototype.
4. **Shoot a mini human-vs-app test.** It makes a strong 10-second clip and is real evidence:
   - Take 20 onions from the shop's pile.
   - Ask the shop owner to sort them into "good" and "not good" by eye. Film their hands only.
   - Photograph the same 20 on the mat with SAMA.
   - Write down both results. A disagreement is fine, and interesting: tap the bulb and show why.
5. **Shoot a mini size check.** Measure 10 onions across their widest point with a ruler or caliper, write the numbers down, then compare them with SAMA's millimetres. This is the first real size-accuracy evidence the project has; the field kit sheet `docs/field-kit/size_truth.csv` is ready for the numbers.
6. **B-roll** (3 to 5 seconds each): the onion heap, a hand picking an onion, a weighing scale, the mat on the counter, and a wide shot of the shop, if allowed.
7. **Light in shops is often yellow or dim.** Stand near the entrance for daylight. If the capture guard refuses, that's honest behaviour: film one refusal, then move to better light.

Afterwards, send the photos and your measurements to the team. They go into `field/` and the evaluation can be re-run from them.

## If something breaks while recording

- **The model is slow on the first photo.** Record the flow once as a warm-up and throw that take away.
- **The camera won't take the photo because of glare or tilt.** Move away from the window, lower the phone, and keep the whole mat in view. After three taps it forces the capture and records that it was forced.
- **The QR won't scan.** Increase phone A's brightness and move phone B back to 20 cm. As a fallback, copy the code text.
- **Anything else:** use the backup lot you completed before recording.
